# Scientific foundation

A recorded physiological difference does not establish overall functional ability. PARALLAX keeps the measurement, its source, a compatible comparison and the scope of a conclusion visible together. NASA resources motivate the questions. Deterministic source-linked templates explain domain and mission relevance while withholding medical conclusions.

[Mulavara et al. (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6133205/), DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000001615, reports physiological and functional changes after spaceflight and bed rest. The cohorts were 13 ISS astronauts, 10 bed-rest controls and 9 bed-rest exercisers. These cohort counts are not exact analysed association-pair counts.

The published changes in estimated medians for Seat Egress and Walk completion time were +31%, +38% and +23% respectively. Recovery from Fall/Stand mean postural sway speed changed +66%, +54% and +42%. These are reported group estimates, not calculations from redistributed participant data. The bed-rest exercise group had no significant reductions in selected lower-body neuromuscular measures while functional task time and sway still changed. Nonsignificance alone does not prove preservation or equivalence, and the cohorts do not establish a causal exercise effect.

Seven signed Somers’ D associations with changes in Seat Egress and Walk completion time are retained:

| Physiological measure              | Somers’ D | Original physiological unit  |
| ---------------------------------- | --------: | ---------------------------- |
| Plasma volume                      |    −0.349 | L                            |
| Lower-body maximum isometric force |    −0.305 | N                            |
| Lower-body maximum power           |    −0.319 | W                            |
| Lower-body total work              |    −0.293 | J                            |
| Dynamic posturography              |    −0.456 | Continuous equilibrium score |
| Tandem walk                        |    −0.428 | % correct steps              |
| Posture-related heart-rate change  |    +0.398 | bpm, standing minus prone    |

D is dimensionless. The primary Results paragraph following Table 3 and its first functional column are the locators. The coefficients describe pooled within-person session changes across all session pairs. They are associations, not causal coefficients, individual predictive accuracy, personal probabilities or engine weights. Negative D indicates discordance and positive D concordance.

The authors used a 1% false-discovery-rate threshold of P < 0.003 for 105 association tests, with a separately described 0.003–0.004 marginal range. The 66 pre/post mixed-model tests use P < 0.008. Table 3's displayed “0.000” values are rounded, never a claim of P = 0. Do not substitute uncorrected P < 0.05 or describe this as Bonferroni correction.

Results/Table 2 and Discussion report greater changes in tasks with greater postural/coordination demand. Activity Board and Hatch Opening did not show significant changes in any cohort. Bed-rest exercisers' Object Translation/Jump Down did not show significant changes, while Seat Egress and Walk, Recovery from Fall/Stand and Ladder Climb difficulties remained. ISS Ladder Climb P = 0.009 does not meet the 0.008 threshold. No omitted magnitudes or confidence intervals are inferred. Exact selected physiological confidence bounds were unavailable from the accessible numerical text; the application does not digitise figures or reconstruct them.

## NASA context

[NASA Astronaut Exercise](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/astronaut-exercise/) describes CEVIS cycling workload and recorded heart rate, ARED resistance equipment, T2 and VO2max testing. [Exercise Countermeasures](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-exercise-countermeasures/) describes exercise systems and monitoring support. These resources support acquisition feasibility. They do not validate the fictional 100 W/final-minute comparison protocol or establish a PARALLAX device connection. Exercise HR, resting HR, posture HR response and aerobic capacity remain separate measures.

[4.0 Human Performance](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-0-human-performance/) and the [Crew Egress Risk report](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/risk_to_vehicle_crew_egress_capability_and_task_performance_as_applied_to_earth_and_extraterrestrial_landings_dag.pdf) explain task and gravity-transition concerns. Neither the fictional task nor a laboratory endpoint measures actual spacecraft egress capability. [Gravity Fields](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-gravity-fields/) and [Distance From Earth](https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-distance-from-earth/) frame the implemented scope. Radiation, isolation and hostile closed environments are recognised but unmodelled.

[6.0 Medical Operations](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/), [3.0 Health and Medical Care](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/3-0-health-and-medical-care/) and the [In-Flight Medical Reference](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo/health-and-medical-systems/in-flight-medical-care/) retain qualified oversight and mission procedures as the care boundary. [Earth-independent medical operations research](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ames/ames-science/ames-space-biosciences/a-clinical-decision-support-system-for-earth-independent-medical-operations/) motivates local capability. PARALLAX implements local evidence review and handoff preparation, without the diagnosis/treatment system described by that research.

Per-value locators, transformations, original units, reuse notes and access limitations are retained in the scientific registers listed in [DATA_SOURCES.md](DATA_SOURCES.md). The [methods](METHODS.md) explain how to reproduce the software arithmetic without private data.

## Mission review context

[Clément et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9733831/), DOI 10.3389/fphys.2022.1029161, assessed functional tasks in 19 astronauts before and after ISS missions returning to Earth. Its Methods and Discussion distinguish instrumented balance and locomotor endpoints. These Earth-return observations motivate a task-specific unresolved question; they do not validate an unsupervised microgravity assessment or transfer a protocol to Mars at 0.38 g.

NASA’s [Human Performance requirements](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-0-human-performance/) distinguish aerobic-capacity requirements from exercise heart rate. V1 4002 specifies an in-mission VO2max of at least 36.5 mL/(min kg) for missions with extraterrestrial surface EVAs; V1 4003 specifies at least 80% of pre-mission aerobic capacity. The fictional mission provides neither appropriate measurement. These are published programme requirements, not PARALLAX heart-rate alarm thresholds or a personal clearance classifier. The mission’s CMO role and delayed Earth link are explicitly hypothetical scenario assumptions, not NASA-approved authority or architecture.

## Assessment acquisition limits

The assessment planner retrieves an observation already collected in a fictional 28 June exercise session. The initial active packet lacks that record; it is not an observation that never occurred. Source identity, 140 bpm, 100 W, final-minute acquisition, original monitor, protocol, session and fictional UTC remain intact. After validation the unchanged evaluator calculates +15 bpm. This is descriptive arithmetic, with no clinical deterioration, cardiovascular danger or fitness inference.

NASA [SM-204](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/gaps/gap.aspx?i=695) is a research gap concerning post-planetary-landing self-administered testing and rehabilitation. It does not supply a validated Mars protocol. Clément's Earth-return tasks cannot be administered in transit as a substitute for post-landing function. The functional requirement remains unresolved after exercise retrieval and outside the approach coverage denominator.

The catalogue also describes potential approved pulse-oximetry and sleep-report workflows and research-only alertness assessment. [FDA pulse-oximeter guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters) identifies device accuracy limitations, including skin-pigmentation effects. [NASA's Fatigue Countermeasures Laboratory](https://www.nasa.gov/human-systems-integration-division/human-performance/fatigue-countermeasures-laboratory/) describes instrument-specific PVT+ and diary research. Neither establishes mission approval for this prototype. No browser reaction test, self-report form, sensor collection or new physical assessment is enabled. These observations cannot replace compatible force or functional evidence.

## Health interpretation and watchlist

The cardiovascular template applies only when the existing evaluator supports the included cycling pair and the mission context is compatible. It describes a higher later recorded HR with matching declared acquisition conditions. The cause is unknown. No physiological cause, fitness deterioration, disease, danger, VO2max change, intervention or suitability for surface work is inferred. Removing/excluding the follow-up or changing applicability immediately withholds the current numerical interpretation.

The force template describes the 1,000 N Earth preflight reference and the need for a compatible follow-up. Mulavara’s distinct neuromuscular and functional endpoints justify retaining separate questions; its Somers’ D coefficients are unchanged and supply no priority weights. The functional template uses Clément’s 19 Earth-return astronauts (walk-and-turn n=7) to explain general balance/locomotor concerns without labelling this fictional astronaut impaired. An applicable supervised Mars protocol remains unresolved. The aerobic template retains the missing method-specific result and the NASA requirement boundary.

`mission/interpretation.ts` records exact source references, populations/acquisition, supported findings, limitations and product consequences. Source-linked watchlist items distinguish individual recorded differences from information gaps and general hazard relevance. They appear in a transparent review order: unresolved activity requirements, supported interpretation, then additional information or monitoring candidates. This order is not clinical triage.

The monitoring designation is a fictional workflow choice for HR review context. Vendor documentation identifies the commercial Astroskin HR/quality channels (19/1000); source time, clock basis, activity, device identity and sample-aligned quality are required context. A vendor HR value can retain a last valid reading during invalid detection. Neither freshness alone nor the historical +15 bpm difference supplies a personalised alarm threshold. The HR/quality adapter and bounded foreground replay process synthetic documented-format data only. Other channel contracts remain preparation; physical hardware and live streams are not connected.

## Phase 8 source additions and product consequences

[Downs et al. (2014), NTRS 20140003747](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140003747) describes peak cycle testing with metabolic gas analysis during ISS missions. PARALLAX therefore keeps oxygen uptake separate from exercise HR. It does not reproduce that study's protocol, validate its invented records or extend results to Mars. The absolute fictional peak observations establish neither a true VO₂max plateau nor mass-relative capacity.

[NASA Standard Measures](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/standard-measures/) includes a separate sleep/circadian research domain. The fictional diary endpoint records reported sleep over equivalent preceding 24-hour periods. It does not diagnose fatigue or predict performance. [NASA Fundamentals of Human Health](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/fundamentals-of-human-health/) and [sleep/circadian/workload risk context](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/risk-of-performance-decrements-and-adverse-health-outcomes-resulting-from-sleep-loss-circadian-desynchronization-and-work-overload/) remain programme context, not validation of diary values.

[NASA Human Performance](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/4-0-human-performance/) and [Exercise Overview](https://www.nasa.gov/ochmo-tb-031-exercise-overview-2/) inform endpoint/resource distinctions. [NASA SM-204](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Gaps/gap.aspx?i=695) retains the research gap for post-planetary-landing self-administered assessment. No validated Mars mobility protocol is invented. Mulavara 2018 and Clément aggregate values, attribution, uncertainty clipping and incompatible-comparison rules are unchanged. Public Research connects these boundaries to the actual planner, scenarios and monitoring workflow.
